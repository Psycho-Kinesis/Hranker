# Logical Reasoning: Blood Relations

## Core Concept
Blood relation questions test your ability to track family relationships through a chain of
statements and correctly identify how two people are related. The key skill is drawing a
quick family tree/diagram rather than trying to hold the relationships in your head.

## Standard Notation for Diagrams
- A horizontal line with "=" or a bracket usually denotes a married couple.
- A vertical line going down denotes a parent-child relationship.
- "+" is commonly used to mark a male, "-" to mark a female, to avoid re-reading pronouns.

## Common Relationship Chains
- Mother's or Father's only son = self (if the speaker is male) or brother.
- Mother's/Father's brother = maternal/paternal uncle.
- Father's sister's husband = uncle (by marriage).
- Son of one's uncle or aunt = cousin.
- Daughter of one's brother or sister = niece.

## Worked Example
"Pointing to a photograph, Rahul said, 'She is the daughter of my grandfather's only son.'
How is the girl in the photo related to Rahul?"
Step 1: Grandfather's only son — since it's the *only* son, this must be Rahul's father
(assuming Rahul is male) or Rahul himself if he has no brothers and is being referred to in
third person, but conventionally this refers to Rahul's father when Rahul is describing his
own family.
Step 2: Daughter of Rahul's father = Rahul's sister.
So the girl in the photograph is Rahul's sister.

## Common Mistakes
1. Not accounting for the possibility that "brother" or "son" in a statement could refer to
   the speaker themselves (as in the "only son" trap above).
2. Losing track of generation levels when a question chains 3-4 relationships together —
   always draw the tree top to bottom by generation.
3. Misreading coded/puzzle-style blood relation questions where relationships are given
   through symbols (e.g., A + B means A is the mother of B) — always decode the full legend
   before attempting the question.
