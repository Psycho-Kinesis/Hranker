# General Studies: Fundamental Rights (Indian Polity)

## Core Concept
Fundamental Rights are enshrined in Part III of the Indian Constitution (Articles 12-35).
They are justiciable, meaning a citizen can directly approach the Supreme Court (Article 32)
or High Courts (Article 226) if these rights are violated.

## The Six Fundamental Rights (Current)
Originally there were seven; the Right to Property was removed as a fundamental right by the
44th Amendment (1978) and now exists only as a legal right under Article 300-A. The current
six are:
1. Right to Equality (Articles 14-18)
2. Right to Freedom (Articles 19-22)
3. Right against Exploitation (Articles 23-24)
4. Right to Freedom of Religion (Articles 25-28)
5. Cultural and Educational Rights (Articles 29-30)
6. Right to Constitutional Remedies (Article 32)

## Key Exam-Relevant Details
- Article 32 is called the "Heart and Soul of the Constitution" by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, because
  it empowers citizens to directly move the Supreme Court for enforcement of Fundamental
  Rights.
- Article 21 (Right to Life and Personal Liberty) has been interpreted expansively by the
  judiciary over time to include rights not explicitly listed in the text — for example, the
  right to privacy, right to a clean environment, and right to a speedy trial, established
  through various Supreme Court judgments.
- Fundamental Rights are not absolute; they are subject to "reasonable restrictions" that the
  State can impose in the interest of sovereignty, public order, decency, or morality.
- Under Article 359, the President can suspend the right to move courts for enforcement of
  Fundamental Rights (except Articles 20 and 21) during a National Emergency.

## Common Mistakes
1. Confusing "Fundamental Rights" (Part III, justiciable) with "Directive Principles of State
   Policy" (Part IV, non-justiciable, meant to guide governance).
2. Forgetting that the Right to Property is no longer a Fundamental Right — a frequently
   tested distractor in prelims-style MCQs.
3. Mixing up which articles are exceptions during an Emergency (Articles 20 and 21 cannot be
   suspended even under Article 359).
