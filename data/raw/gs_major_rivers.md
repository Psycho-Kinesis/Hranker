# General Studies: Major Rivers of India

## Core Concept
Indian rivers are broadly classified into two systems: Himalayan rivers (perennial, fed by
snowmelt and rain) and Peninsular rivers (mostly seasonal, rain-fed, flowing over older, more
stable rock).

## Himalayan River System
- The Ganga originates from the Gangotri glacier (Gaumukh) in Uttarakhand and is joined by
  major tributaries like the Yamuna, Ghaghara, Gandak, and Kosi before draining into the Bay
  of Bengal via the Sundarbans delta, which it shares with the Brahmaputra.
- The Indus originates near Lake Mansarovar in Tibet and flows through Ladakh before entering
  Pakistan; despite originating in India, most of its course lies outside the country.
- The Brahmaputra originates in Tibet (where it is called the Tsangpo), enters India through
  Arunachal Pradesh, and is known for significant braiding and flooding in Assam.

## Peninsular River System
- The Narmada and Tapi are unusual among peninsular rivers because they flow westward into
  the Arabian Sea through rift valleys, rather than eastward like most peninsular rivers.
- The Godavari, often called the "Ganga of the South," is the largest peninsular river and
  flows eastward into the Bay of Bengal.
- The Krishna and Kaveri are the other major east-flowing peninsular rivers, both important
  for irrigation and both subjects of long-running inter-state water-sharing disputes.

## Key Exam-Relevant Details
- Rivers flowing into the Bay of Bengal: Ganga, Brahmaputra, Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna,
  Kaveri.
- Rivers flowing into the Arabian Sea: Indus, Narmada, Tapi, and the smaller west-flowing
  rivers of the Western Ghats.
- The "doab" (land between two rivers) between the Ganga and Yamuna is one of the most
  agriculturally significant and densely populated regions in India.

## Common Mistakes
1. Confusing the direction of flow for the Narmada/Tapi (westward) versus other peninsular
   rivers (eastward) — one of the most commonly tested facts in this topic.
2. Mixing up the origin points of the Ganga (Gangotri, India) and the Brahmaputra/Indus
   (Tibet) — all three eventually flow through India but only the Ganga originates within it.
3. Forgetting which sea a river drains into when a question lists several rivers together and
   asks to group them.
