# Quantitative Aptitude: Time and Work

## Core Concept
Time and Work problems are built on one idea: work done is the product of the rate of work
and the time spent. If a person can complete a task in N days, their one-day work rate is
1/N of the task.

## The "Total Work = LCM" Technique
Instead of working with fractions throughout, assume the total work equals the LCM of all
the given time values. This converts every rate into a whole number of "units of work per
day," which is much faster to manipulate under exam time pressure.

Example: If A can finish a job in 12 days and B can finish it in 18 days, take total work =
LCM(12, 18) = 36 units.
- A's rate = 36 / 12 = 3 units/day
- B's rate = 36 / 18 = 2 units/day
- Working together, they complete 3 + 2 = 5 units/day
- Together they finish the job in 36 / 5 = 7.2 days

## Efficiency and Inverse Proportion
Time taken is inversely proportional to efficiency. If A is twice as efficient as B, A takes
half the time B takes for the same task. This inverse relationship is frequently tested by
giving efficiency ratios instead of direct time values.

## Pipes and Cisterns (Same Family of Problems)
Pipes and cisterns questions use identical logic, with one twist: an outlet (drain) pipe does
negative work. If pipe A fills a tank in 6 hours and pipe B (a drain) empties it in 9 hours,
using total work = LCM(6, 9) = 18 units:
- A's rate = +3 units/hour, B's rate = -2 units/hour
- Combined rate = +1 unit/hour → tank fills in 18 hours if both are open.

## Common Mistakes
1. Adding time values directly instead of adding rates (rates add, times do not).
2. Forgetting to treat an emptying pipe or an inefficient worker who "spoils work" as
   negative work.
3. Misreading combined-work questions where people join or leave partway through — these
   require splitting the timeline into segments and tracking work done in each segment
   separately.
